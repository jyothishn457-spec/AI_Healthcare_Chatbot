COMMON MEDICINES GUIDE
This guide gives general information about common medicines. Doses must always be prescribed by a doctor. Never exceed the recommended dose and never combine medicines without medical advice.

PARACETAMOL (ACETAMINOPHEN)
Paracetamol is a pain reliever and fever reducer. It is used for headaches, muscle aches and fever. Adults should not take more than 3000 to 4000 mg per day. Taking too much can damage the liver. Do not combine it with other products that also contain paracetamol.

IBUPROFEN
Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used for pain, inflammation and fever. Take it with food to reduce stomach upset. It should be used with caution in people with stomach ulcers, kidney disease or heart disease. Always follow the package instructions or your doctor's advice.

METFORMIN
Metformin is the first-line oral medicine for type 2 diabetes. It helps lower blood sugar and is usually taken with meals to reduce stomach side effects. Rarely, it can cause a serious condition called lactic acidosis. Regular kidney function tests are recommended while taking it.

AMLODIPINE
Amlodipine is a calcium channel blocker used to treat high blood pressure and chest pain (angina). Common side effects include ankle swelling, flushing and headache. Take it at the same time each day and do not stop suddenly without medical advice.

AMOXICILLIN
Amoxicillin is an antibiotic used for bacterial infections such as ear, chest and throat infections. Complete the entire course even if you feel better. It does not work for viral infections. Tell your doctor if you are allergic to penicillin.
