DIABETES MELLITUS
Diabetes is a chronic condition in which the body cannot properly regulate blood sugar (glucose). Common symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, extreme fatigue, blurred vision and slow-healing wounds. Type 1 diabetes requires daily insulin. Type 2 diabetes is often managed with lifestyle changes, oral medicines and sometimes insulin. Regular blood glucose monitoring, a balanced diet low in refined sugars, and regular physical activity are all important. Always follow the advice of your doctor or endocrinologist.

HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
Hypertension is persistently elevated blood pressure (140/90 mmHg or higher). It often has no symptoms, which is why it is called the "silent killer". Risk factors include a high salt intake, obesity, smoking, stress and a family history of the condition. Management includes reducing salt, regular exercise, weight control, limiting alcohol and taking prescribed antihypertensive medicines as directed. Untreated hypertension raises the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney damage.

ASTHMA
Asthma is a condition in which the airways narrow and swell, causing wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness and coughing, especially at night or early in the morning. Common triggers include allergens, cold air, smoke and exercise. Inhalers are the mainstay of treatment: bronchodilators give quick relief, and corticosteroid inhalers control the condition over time. People with asthma should carry their reliever inhaler and follow an action plan agreed with their doctor.

COMMON COLD AND FLU (INFLUENZA)
The common cold causes a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, a sore throat and a mild cough. The flu is usually more severe, with a high fever, body aches, headache and fatigue. Both are viral infections, so antibiotics do not help. Treatment focuses on rest, fluids and over-the-counter medicines to ease symptoms. Annual flu vaccination helps prevent seasonal influenza.

IRON DEFICIENCY ANEMIA
Anemia means there are not enough healthy red blood cells to carry oxygen around the body. Iron deficiency anemia causes tiredness, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath and dizziness. It is common in women with heavy periods and in people with poor diets. Treatment includes iron-rich foods such as leafy green vegetables, legumes and lean red meat, plus iron supplements prescribed by a doctor.
